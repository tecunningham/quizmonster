# Image credits and curation

Reviewed 2026-10-17. These images retain their individual source licenses. Local display copies are resized to at most 800 pixels, converted to JPEG and stripped of embedded metadata. Attribution remains in the game, answer history, credits page and this file.

The catalogue contains five examples in each of 16 categories. Labels are curated from named artworks/monuments and linked subject references, explicit dated fashion captions, and species descriptions. Commons category membership and search-result ranking are not treated as proof. Some buildings have later additions; Aztec-associated sites can predate Aztec rule. This is a small teaching collection, not an exhaustive or expert taxonomy.

## African bush elephants

- **Desert-adapted elephant dust bathing in Damaraland, Namibia. Desert-adapted elephants often dust-bathe by spraying sand or dust over their bodies with their trunks. This behavior helps protect their skin from intense sun and heat, acts as a natural insect repellent, and creates a protective layer that reduces moisture loss in the harsh desert environment. Desert-adapted elephants are the same species than African bush elephants (Loxodonta africana) but have behavior and physical adaptations for life in extreme desert environments. They are generally slimmer and slightly smaller than typical bush elephants and weighing toward the lower end of the species’ range due to limited food availability. Although African bush elephants can live 60–70 years in the wild, desert-living populations often have shorter average lifespans (40-50 years) because of the harsh conditions they live in.** (`images/image-71.jpg`)
